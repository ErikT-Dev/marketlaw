# How to play MarketLaw

You are the mayor of a new city. Play cards to build homes, industry, services and public
places, turn your city's output into income, and finish ahead of the other mayors.

## The goal

Each player builds their own **city**: all the cards you have played. When the game ends, the
player with the highest score wins (see [The end of the game](#the-end-of-the-game)).

The game ends after the turn in which a player reaches the **end goal**: **income of at least
22** and all **3 projects** completed.

Everyone plays the same turn at the same time. A turn's main phase ends when every player is
done, so if you are quick you may wait a little for the others.

## Your stats

| Stat | You start with | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Funds | 7 | Your money. You pay for cards with it. |
| Income | 3 | The funds you get at the end of every turn. You need 22 for the end goal. |
| Influence | 0 | You get 1 every turn. Spend it on [influence actions](#influence). |
| Workforce | 0 | Made by Residential cards, used by all other cards. |
| Quality | 0 | Made by Public cards, used by the other sectors. |
| Satisfaction | 0 | Made by Public cards, used by the other sectors. |
| Product | 0 | Made by Industry cards. Becomes [income](#income). |
| Distribution | 0 | Made by Service cards. Becomes [income](#income). |
| Bonus | 0 | Grows every turn and turns into extra funds (see [Bonus](#bonus)). |

Workforce, quality and satisfaction can drop below zero. **While any of them is negative, you
cannot gain income or complete projects or quests**, so keep them balanced.

Turns are numbered from 1, the same for everyone.

## The cards

There are 240 cards, all different. Each card has:

- a **sector**: Residential, Industry, Service or Public
- a **tier**: 1, 2 or 3. Higher tiers are stronger and cost more.
- a **cost** in funds
- **stat numbers**
- 1 to 5 **types**, such as `Farm`, `Low Wealth` or `Retail`. Some cards count a type twice.
- up to 4 **combos** (see [Types and combos](#types-and-combos))

What a card does when you play it depends on its sector:

| Sector | Gives you | Uses up |
|---|---|---|
| Residential | workforce | quality and satisfaction |
| Industry | product | workforce, quality and satisfaction |
| Service | distribution | workforce, quality and satisfaction |
| Public | quality and satisfaction | workforce |

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | 3–6 | 7–9 | 11–13 |
| Product or distribution | 1 | 2 | 3 |

A Public card is a **quality card** if it gives more quality than satisfaction, and a
**satisfaction card** otherwise. Some draws and marketplace places use one of 5 **kinds**:
Residential, Industry, Service, Public quality card or Public satisfaction card.

You can look at every card in the game in the **Card set**, from the menu.

**Real cost:** a card costs 1 more for every card you already have in your city of the same
sector and tier. *Example: you already have two tier 1 Industry cards. A third one costs 2 more
than printed.*

## How a turn works

Turn 1 has no start-of-turn steps and no market phase, because everyone starts with a full
hand: it begins with the main phase.

Every turn after that has three parts:

1. **Start of the turn:** the marketplace and quests are refreshed, then the **market phase**,
   where each player in turn draws cards, one at a time.
2. **Main phase:** everyone plays at the same time, each in their own city. Your main phase
   starts as soon as your own market go is over, while later players still have their go.
3. **End of the turn:** once everyone is done, income, influence and bonus are paid out.

**Who goes first:** at the start of the game, players get seats 1, 2, 3… in a random order.
Seat 1 goes first in turn 1, seat 2 in turn 2, and so on, round and round. The order in a turn
starts from that turn's first player and goes up by seat number. Players who have left the game
are skipped.

## The main phase

During the main phase you can, in any order and as often as allowed:

- **play a card** from your hand (see [Playing a card](#playing-a-card))
- **sell** cards from your hand for 1 fund each. You can sell several cards at once.
- **claim all** combos that are ready (see [Types and combos](#types-and-combos))
- use **influence actions** (see [Influence](#influence))
- complete **projects** (see [Projects](#projects))
- complete **quests** (see [Quests](#quests))
- **undo** (see [Undo](#undo))

Then you **end your turn**.

You can take up to **50 actions** per main phase. Undo counts as an action; ending your turn
does not, and is always allowed.

**What you see:** your own moves show straight away. Other players' moves stay hidden until
the main phase ends; until then, you see the other players as they were when their own main
phase began, plus who is done or out of time. The deck and the discard pile also look as they
did when your main phase began, except for your own draws and the market goes after it. Only the end result of each player's main phase is shown, never the
moves they undid. Everyone's time bank counts down live.

## Playing a card

You can play a card when:

- you have enough funds for its real cost
- it is tier 1, or it is turn 5 or later
- it is your first card this turn, or you have a **build pass** to spend on an extra card
- you have not used "skip build" this turn

When you play it, you pay its real cost, it joins your city, its stat numbers are applied, its
types are added to your type counts, and its combos become available.

## Undo

You can take back your own moves in the main phase, one at a time, newest first. An undo puts
back everything the move changed, including the price of your next bought build pass. You can
undo:

- playing a card
- claim all
- influence actions that don't draw a card
- completing a quest whose reward doesn't draw a card

Nothing that draws a card can be undone, and neither can selling, completing a project or ending
your turn. Once one of those has happened, nothing before it can be undone either.

## Ending your turn

When you end your turn you are **done** for this turn; you can't take more actions. Any combos
that are ready are claimed for you first, so you never lose one by ending your turn. The main
phase ends when every player is done.

The order in which players became done is remembered: it breaks ties at the end of the game.
Players whose time runs out at the same moment become done in player order.

## Time

| Turn | 1 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 and later |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main phase time limit | 3:30 | 3:55 | 4:27 | 4:58 | 5:30 |

The main phase limit grows by about 6 seconds each turn, and counts from the start of your own
main phase. Each go in the market phase has a limit of 40 seconds.

You also have a **time bank of 15 minutes** for the whole game. The first 75 seconds of every
main phase, and the first 5 seconds of every market go, are free. After that, your bank counts
down until you end your turn or make your market choice. When your bank is empty, you only have
the free time. Everyone can see everyone's bank.

**When your time runs out:** in the main phase, you are **out of time**, but you can keep
playing. You are made done automatically, with your ready combos claimed, once every other
player is done or out of time too. On a market go, see [The marketplace](#the-marketplace). In a
ranked game, if your time runs out in the main phase in 3 turns in a row, you are treated as
having left the game; each time it happens, you are told how many times in a row it has been so
far. In a casual game nobody leaves because of time-outs.

**Paused game:** if someone's time would run out while nobody in the game is connected, the game
pauses. It continues as soon as anyone reconnects, and the player whose time was running out
gets their free time again. A game paused for 24 hours is abandoned: it ends with no winner,
and ratings stay the same.

## The end of every turn

After the main phase, in this order:

1. Every player gets +1 influence, gets their income in funds, and adds their bonus (see
   [Bonus](#bonus)). Players who were among the first to complete a project this turn get their
   extra 2 funds (see [Projects](#projects)). Unused build passes are kept.
2. Quests that someone completed this turn leave the table for good. Quests that have been on
   the table for 5 turns without being completed leave it too, and go back into the quest list
   after the next turn's empty places are filled, so they cannot come straight back.
3. The next turn begins.

## The start of every turn

From turn 2 on, in this order:

1. The marketplace removes cards that have been on display for 2 market phases and fills its
   empty places.
2. Empty quest places are filled. From turn 20 there are 5 quest places instead of 4.
3. The market phase, including everyone's turn draw.

## Types and combos

Your **type count** is how many of each type your city has. A card that counts a type twice adds
2. Types gained from quests count too.

Each card has up to 4 **combos**, which become available once the card is in your city. A combo
needs either:

- **a card name**, such as "Stone Quarry": it can be claimed once, as soon as that card is also
  in your city. It doesn't matter which of the two you played first.
- **a type count**, such as "2 `Farm`": it can be claimed once when you have that many.
  **Infinite** combos can be claimed again every time you reach the next multiple of their
  count: for "2 `Farm`" at 2, 4, 6 and so on.

A combo gives +1 or more workforce, quality or satisfaction. You claim every ready combo at once
with **claim all**. *Example: a "2 `Farm`" infinite combo, not claimed yet, with 4 `Farm` in your
city, is claimed twice.*

## Income

Whenever you have **at least 1 product and 1 distribution**, and workforce, quality and
satisfaction are all **0 or more**, this happens by itself:

- 1 product and 1 distribution are used up
- your income goes up by 1
- you draw 1 card of a random sector

This repeats as long as it can, and is checked after every action.

## Bonus

At the end of every turn, your bonus grows by:

workforce + quality + satisfaction + 4 × product + 3 × distribution

Negative stats lower it, and bonus can go below zero. Every full 10 bonus then turns into
1 fund. *Example: bonus 23 becomes 2 funds, and 3 bonus stays.*

## Influence

Influence buys optional trades. Some can only be used a limited number of times per game, and
you need enough of everything an action costs.

| Action | Times per game |
|---|---|
| 4 influence → 2 funds | 1 |
| 4 influence → 1 quality | 1 |
| 4 influence → 1 satisfaction | 1 |
| 6 influence → 1 workforce | 1 |
| 1 fund → 1 influence | any |
| 2 funds → 1 build pass (costs 1 more fund each time you buy one) | any |
| 5 influence → 1 build pass | 1 |
| Skip build (before you play a card this turn): no more cards this turn, but you get a build pass for later | 3 |
| 1 influence + 1 quality → 1 satisfaction | 1 |
| 1 influence + 1 satisfaction → 1 quality | 1 |
| 2 influence + 1 product → 1 distribution + 1 satisfaction | 1 |
| 2 influence + 1 distribution + 1 satisfaction → 1 product | 1 |
| 2 influence + 1 quality → 1 workforce | 1 |
| 2 influence + 1 satisfaction → 1 workforce | 1 |
| 1 workforce → 1 satisfaction | 1 |
| 1 workforce → 1 quality | 1 |
| 2 quality → 1 satisfaction | any |
| 2 satisfaction → 1 quality | any |
| 4 quality → 1 workforce | any |
| 4 satisfaction → 1 workforce | any |
| 3 influence → draw a tier 1 card of a kind you choose | any |
| 4 influence → draw a tier 2 card of a kind you choose | any |
| 5 influence → draw a tier 3 card of a kind you choose | any |

You can only use a draw action while the deck or discard pile, as you see them, still has a
card of that kind and tier. If other players took the last ones earlier in the same main phase,
you get the nearest other tier instead, and the influence is still spent.

## Projects

Each player has 3 projects, and can complete each one once:

| Project | Needs |
|---|---|
| Clean up the streets | 8 quality |
| Culture campaign | 8 satisfaction |
| Help the homeless | 8 workforce |

You need the amount, and workforce, quality and satisfaction must all be 0 or more. Completing a
project uses up those 8, gives **+1 income, +2 funds and +3 influence**, and draws 1 card. Your
third project gives another +1 income and 1 more card.

**Be first:** in the first turn in which anyone completes a project, everyone who completes that
project in the same turn gets **+2 funds** extra at the end of the turn.

## The deck and drawing cards

Everyone draws from **one shared deck** of all 240 cards. Sold cards and cards leaving the
marketplace go to a shared **discard pile**. You see how many cards it holds, but not which
cards. You can hold any number of cards.

Every draw asks for a sector (or a kind) and a tier. The sector is either chosen or random.
The tier is either fixed by the draw or random, with **higher tiers more likely later in the
game**:

| Turn | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100% | 0% | 0% |
| 5 | 71% | 29% | 0% |
| 9 | 43% | 57% | 0% |
| 13 | 14% | 67% | 19% |
| 17 | 0% | 58% | 42% |
| 21 | 0% | 34% | 66% |
| 25 | 0% | 11% | 89% |
| 29 and later | 0% | 0% | 100% |

If the deck has no card of that sector and tier, those cards are shuffled back in from the
discard pile. If there are none there either, you get the nearest other tier (the lower one
first). Only if the whole sector or kind is gone do you get any card at all.

Other players see who drew how many cards, and the sector and tier asked for, but not which
cards. During the main phase, sold cards wait in a holding pile and only reach the discard pile
when the phase ends.

**Starting hand:** 7 cards: one tier 1 card of each sector, plus three cards of random sectors:
one tier 1, one tier 1 or 2, and one of any tier.

## The marketplace

The marketplace shows **8 cards** that everyone can see: one place for each of the 5 kinds, and
3 open places with a random sector.

**Market phase:** from turn 2 on, every player gets **one go**, in player order. Your go is your
**turn draw**. Choose one:

- **(A) Take a free card** from the marketplace. You also draw 1 card of the same sector from the
  deck.
- **(B) Skip the marketplace:** choose a sector and draw 2 cards of it from the deck.

If the marketplace is empty by your go, you take (B). You still pay a card's real cost when you
play it. Cards you got this turn, in the market or later in the turn, are marked **new**.

**Time:** if your time runs out on your go, the next player's go starts and your main phase
begins. You can no longer take a marketplace card, but at any time in your main phase you choose
a sector and draw 2 cards of it. If you end your turn without choosing, a random sector is used.

Taken places stay empty until the next turn. A card stays in the marketplace for at most 2
market phases.

## Quests

The **quests table** has 4 quests (5 from turn 20) that everyone can complete. The game starts
with 4 tier 1 quests; tier 2 quests can appear from turn 5, tier 3 from turn 13.

- A quest asks you to **have** something right now, such as a stat amount, a type count or
  certain cards in your city. Nothing is used up. Workforce, quality and satisfaction must all be
  0 or more.
- Completing a quest is a main-phase action, and you get the reward at once.
- Each player can complete each quest once, and any number of players can complete it in the
  same turn.
- A completed quest leaves the table at the end of that turn.
- A quest stays at most 5 turns. If nobody completed it, it goes back into the quest list, but
  not in time for the next turn's refill.

## The quest list

- Every requirement means "at least".
- "3 Residential cards" counts cards in your city.
- "1 `Low Wealth`" counts your type count, including types gained from quests.
- Card counts and types are checked separately. *Example: "2 Service cards and 1 `Service` type"
  needs 2 Service cards and a `Service` type count of 1, from any card.*
- **Draw** rewards draw each card from a random sector.
- **Gain a type** adds 1 to that type count, as if another card in your city had that type. It
  counts for type combos, bonus types and quests, but it is not a card.

## The end of the game

The game ends after the main phase of the turn in which a player still in the game reaches the
end goal (income 22 and all 3 projects). That turn's end-of-turn payouts are skipped, except the
extra funds for being first to a project. If nobody reaches the end goal, the game ends after
turn 40's main phase.

**Score:**

```
870
− 25   × (turn number − 1)
+ 6.5  × income
+ 0.7  × workforce
+ 0.55 × quality
+ 0.5  × satisfaction
+ 2.75 × product
+ 2.25 × distribution
+ 0.2  × (funds + cards in hand)
+ bonus-type points
```

The result is rounded down to a whole number. The "turn number" part is the same for every
player in a game; it makes scores comparable between games.

**Bonus types:** at the start of every game, 2 different types are picked at random. At the end,
you score points for every count of those types in your type counts, including types gained
from quests:

| Points per count | Types |
|---|---|
| 2 | `Suburban Housing`, `Low Wealth`, `Town Housing`, `Luxury`, `City Housing`, `Light Industry`, `Heavy Industry`, `High Tech`, `Service`, `Leisure`, `Retail`, `Culture`, `Office` |
| 3 | `Raw Material`, `Forestry`, `Farm`, `Power Plant`, `Mine`, `Food`, `Sea`, `Utility`, `Mass Transit`, `Health`, `Mercantile`, `Beautification`, `Safety`, `Education` |
| 4 | `Alcohol`, `Grain`, `Petroleum`, `Mass Media`, `Finance`, `Rental Space` |

**Winner:** players are ranked by score, and the highest score wins. Players who left the game
come last. On a tie, the player who became done earlier in the final turn places higher; if
that is also equal, the one earlier in that turn's player order.
